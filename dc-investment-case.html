Mission-Critical: The Investment Case for Repositioning Mukilteo from Commercial Glazing to Data Center Enclosures

An evidence-based case for an 18-month, tranche-gated pivot — with the bear case priced, the downside capped, and every number traceable to a primary source.

1The legacy market will not recover

Three independent lines of evidence converge on "structural," not "cyclical"

Peer-reviewed economics has closed the question the historical Mukilteo backlog depends on. Downtown Seattle office vacancy hit 35.8% in Q2 2026 — worst of any major U.S. downtown — and the regional construction pipeline is down 99% from its 2023 peak. This is not a sentiment cycle; it is a capital-markets fact.

Submarket (Q2 2026)VacancyValue destruction

The capital freeze is the binding mechanism

Roughly $2.5 trillion of U.S. commercial real-estate debt matures 2025–2027 — and office cannot clear the financing committee: only 32% of maturing office conduit loans refinanced in 2024, versus roughly 85% for industrial, multifamily, and retail (IMF). NBER data puts 44% of office loans in negative equity with 64% facing a debt-service-coverage shortfall. Office CMBS delinquency hit an all-time record of 12.34% in January 2026, above the GFC-era peak (Trepp). 2025 CMBS workouts cleared at a median 53% discount to origination value, with office carrying more than half that balance.

The one asset class escaping the freeze: the same credit system that will not finance an office tower committed $121 billion to U.S. data-center properties in 2025, and data-center ABS/CMBS issuance more than doubled to ~$26 billion. Capital is not leaving construction — it is rotating toward assets whose demand is contracted, pre-leased, and balance-sheet-backed.

Source: Kidder Mathews & Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2026 MarketBeat; King County Assessor via Seattle Times; Gupta/Mittal/Van Nieuwerburgh, American Economic Review 116(2) 2026; MBA maturity survey; Trepp CMBS Delinquency Report (Jan 2026); IMF Global Financial Stability Report; NBER WP 31970; S&P Global data-center credit tracking.

4Why we win · the honest technical case

We sell code compliance, moisture/ESD risk control, and RFS-date insurance — not energy savings

DOE/LBNL modeling puts envelope loads at ≤1% of data-center cooling energy. No credible pitch survives claiming savings there — so this proposal doesn't make one, and that restraint is what earns credibility with hyperscaler engineering teams who check the same numbers.

IMP core R-value vs. panel thickness, against WSEC 2021 wall requirements

Dashed lines mark Washington's prescriptive wall-R requirements (metal building R-27, steel-framed R-23). A 4-inch premium-core (QuadCore) panel clears both with margin; 3-inch PIR misses both.

What we claim

  • Compliance — passing WSEC/IECC on the first submission, with continuous insulation and factory-sealed joints.
  • Risk physics — airtightness controls interior moisture and electrostatic discharge, the actual uptime threats.
  • Tested performance — 0.40 cfm/ft² @75Pa code baseline, 0.25 on delivered IMP scope, verifiable by blower door at turnover.

What we deliberately don't claim

The original draft's energy-savings framing. It does not survive DOE arithmetic against modern liquid-cooled rack densities, and saying so up front is a credibility signal, not a weakness — it's exactly the discount a technical reviewer applies to a pitch that claims it anyway.

Source: DOE/LBNL data-center energy assessments; WSEC 2021 Table C402.1.3; ASTM E779/E3158; ASHRAE 90.4/TC 9.9; ABAA/USACE airtightness protocols.

5Why we win · the operational case

Prefab compresses schedules 20–50%, and delay costs up to $100K/day

Unitized curtain wall is prefab enclosure — shop-fabricated panels, crane-set installation, engineered anchors, field air/water testing. The craft stack IMP erection needs is the one Mukilteo already runs.

Conventional vs. modular data-center delivery, months to weathertight

What transfers directly

Manufacturer installer training (CENTRIA/Kingspan/AWIP), IMP-specific joint detailing, FM/ASTM E119 fire-assembly familiarity, and ABAA airtightness-field protocol — all modest, certifiable additions on a shop that already runs thermal-break gasketing and tolerance-critical QA/QC.

The bid we're actually making

Not "we are cheaper." It's "we hold your RFS date" — backed by a timed mock-up, published crew productivity, and LD-aware contract language the GCs in this market already price.

Source: peer-reviewed prefabrication/lean-construction literature; McKinsey modular construction (2019); liquidated-damages norms per proposal Ch. 6.

The moat, stated mechanically

  • Certified prefab — manufacturer installer agreements are quota-limited and slow to obtain; each one held is one a fast-follower doesn't have.
  • Union jurisdiction — Locals 516/86/29/14 span both corridors under one regional master agreement; signatory GCs need signatory envelope subs.
  • Tested performance — a published 0.25 cfm/ft² blower-door result on delivered scope compounds as a reference asset. It cannot be claimed, only earned.

Honest boundary

None of this wins the first job by itself. The first award comes from a GC with a staffing hole and an RFS date it cannot move — the job in Phase 1–2 is to be prequalified, certified, and standing next to that hole when it opens.

9The decision

The asymmetry favors acting now

The cost of entry is bounded and resalable; the cost of waiting is a shrinking market and a crowded doorway. Pick a scenario:

The loss is certification-scale spend, partially resalable, with the legacy curtain-wall platform fully intact. Tranches 1 and 2 are the only capital at risk before a signed subcontract exists.

We hold manufacturer certifications, tested references, and GC prequalification in a market where 80.4% of construction is pre-leased and the erector bench is the binding constraint — assets that took incumbents years to assemble and a fast-follower cannot shortcut.

The same evidence guarantees the doorway fills: certification quotas, union crew availability, and GC shortlists are all finite — and the office pipeline that's supposed to carry us in the meantime has already contracted 99% from its 2023 peak.

Three approvals requested this quarter

  • Approve Tranche 1 certification funding and the 18-month phase calendar with its published gates.
  • Authorize execution of manufacturer installer agreements (CENTRIA / Kingspan / AWIP) and ABAA/BECx training enrollments.
  • Approve the tranche-release protocol: documented evidence against the trigger table, GM signature, ownership review — kill criteria adopted as written, before the first dollar moves.
"The question before ownership is not whether the data center boom is real — the financing system has already answered that. It is whether this company's capacity follows the capital that is building, or stays with the capital that is leaving."
