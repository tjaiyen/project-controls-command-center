A unitized facade package is a production line with an installation window

A critical‑path schedule tracks activities. This package lives or dies on panel flow — how fast the shop finishes units, how fast the field sets them, and how many crated units sit between the two. Everything on this page is computed from one panel ledger at render time; nothing below is a stored result or a typed figure.

Synthetic illustrative data. The tower, the elevations, the panel counts, the survey readings and the cost detail are invented to exercise the method. No client, employer or project data appears anywhere on this page — not from Walters & Wolf, not from any other former employer. Figures marked illustrative are round assumptions rather than ledger‑derived values, and say so where they appear.

Factory output, field setting, and the buffer between them

The single most useful number on a unitized job is not percent complete — it is how many working days of crated panels are standing between the shop and the setting crew. When that number falls, the crane goes idle three weeks later. It is a leading indicator with real lead time, and it is computed here rather than asserted.

Earned value, when physical completion is actually countable

Most earned‑value disputes come down to an argued percent complete. Unitized work has an advantage stick‑built does not: panels are countable. Earned value here is panel counts times a published rule of credit — not a superintendent's judgement, and not a cost‑percentage standing in for physical progress.

Cost‑variance decomposition, by elevation

Which elevations are carrying the overrun, ranked. A package‑level CPI tells you there is a problem; this tells you where to walk.

Monte Carlo — cost at completion

Ten thousand runs. Each elevation's remaining work is drawn from a three‑point range around its own observed cost efficiency, so an elevation running well keeps its advantage in the draw instead of being averaged away. Fixed seed — the same page reload gives the same distribution.

What the bid assumed, against what the job did

A single margin variance is a symptom with at least three different diseases. Splitting it into quantity, price and productivity is what makes it actionable — a quantity miss is an estimating or scope problem, a price miss is a procurement problem, a productivity miss is a field or shop problem, and the fixes are not the same. The first two decompose exactly; the identity is asserted in the page's own test harness.

Tolerance creep — a cost indicator disguised as a survey reading

Setting variance at each vertical joint is individually trivial and cumulatively not. A consistent bias of a fraction of a millimetre per panel compounds across an elevation sweep until the perimeter joint no longer has room for sealant — at which point the remedy is rework, and it is already paid for. Surveying module width every few panels turns that from a discovery into a forecast.

What this page deliberately does not do

  • It does not produce a critical‑path schedule. There is no logic network here, no float calculation off real activity ties, and no claim to either. Panel flow and takt are production measures; they inform a schedule, they do not replace one.
  • It does not forecast a completion date. The Monte Carlo runs on cost, where the three‑point ranges are defensible. A date distribution would need duration uncertainty this synthetic ledger does not carry, so it is declined rather than faked.
  • It does not price the tolerance risk. Creep is shown as a physical projection against a joint budget. Converting millimetres into a rework dollar figure would require a remediation unit rate I have no verified source for, so the page stops at the physical reading and says why.
  • It carries no real project data. Every figure is synthetic. The method is the content.

Verification

Two independent checks, both runnable:

  • node verify-facade.cjs — stubs the DOM, executes this page's own script, then re‑derives every package total from the raw ledger fields rather than reading the computed values back. Reading a number back only proves it was stored.
  • The same harness asserts the quantity + price = total identity on the bid‑versus‑actual decomposition, and that the shop and field views tie to the same package earned value. Those are the two places this method could silently be wrong.
