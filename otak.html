2What I don't bring, stated once

The shortfalls behind the partials

Seven lines above are marked Partial. Two things account for nearly all of them, and one line is an outright gap. Here they are without the hedging.

The partials  Owning the CPM schedule

I have built the duration inputs that set a bid's schedule — on Bellevue 600, I calculated crew installation rates against glass‑panel square‑footage takeoffs to establish total installation weeks; at Pillari Bros., I converted quantity takeoffs (pipe linear footage, excavation yardage) into crew‑production‑rate duration estimates for NJDOT and municipal bids. At B.E. Meyers I took on program coordination for a new contract, identifying the actions that had to complete in sequence for the production ramp to hold. What I have not done is own the live critical‑path model itself — administering a scheduling tool, tracking float, updating the schedule during execution — on a major capital program. That is a real gap and it is the first thing I would close.

Gap  Leading a $500M+ program

I have worked at that scale without having led at it. Cost intelligence I built at Collins Aerospace informed pricing and sourcing across $500M+ of annual production; the standard‑cost system I built at B.E. Meyers supported $100M+ in total contract value; the curtain‑wall packages I priced ran past $50M each. That is exposure to the scale, not leadership at it. The posting's actual bar is leading significant portions of such projects rather than running one outright, and it lists this as preferred rather than required — but I still can't claim it, so I won't.

Partial  Two more worth naming

  • Professional Engineer — I hold the Engineer in Training in Civil, not the full license. Preferred, not required.
  • Direct reports — the posting asks for mentoring junior staff, which I've done; I have led through influence and process rather than headcount, and have not formally managed a team.

4First 90 days

Where I'd start

Written against what Otak's Project Controls practice actually does — contract‑package reporting across major capital transportation and infrastructure programs.

Days 1–30 · Learn the schedule, not just the cost
Sit with the schedulers first. My cost and reporting side transfers on day one; the CPM model is the part I need to earn. Walk every contract package's control account structure, learn how earned value is being claimed, and find where the reporting is manual today.
Days 31–60 · Close the reporting gap
Take the most manual recurring report and rebuild it as something that refreshes itself. This is the work I've done from zero twice; it is usually where a controls practice is losing the most analyst hours, and it is the fastest visible win that doesn't require me to be the expert on the program yet.
Days 61–90 · Make the variance legible upward
Get to one view leadership actually opens — package‑level cost and schedule performance with the drill‑down intact, so a portfolio average never hides a single package the way it does in the demo. Then start mentoring on the reporting layer, which is the part I can teach immediately.
